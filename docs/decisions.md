# Open Decisions

Decisions the build has defaulted conservatively. Base Recruits is owned by the founder and Jonathan Caceres; it is a
separate company from any other venture. Each default is implemented, tested and
changeable with one migration or config edit. Recommended answers are engineering recommendations, not legal advice.

## Founder decisions

| # | Decision | Current default | Trade-off | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Supabase project for Base Recruits development | **Decided 2026-10-22; created 2026-10-23 by the founder:** project "Base Recruits" (us-east-2) in its own Base Recruits organization (Pro). Migrations deploy from `main` through Supabase's GitHub integration; preview branching off (`docs/deployment.md`) | On the Pro plan every member of an organization can open every project in it, so anyone added for Base Recruits also sees the other projects there | Done. An unused empty project in the same organization (ca-central-1) should be deleted by the founder
| F2 | Who reviews coach verifications, and how fast | **Decided 2026-10-22: Adam Concadoro (founder) reviews coach verifications.** Backup reviewer and turnaround target not yet set | Every verified coach is a manual review; slow reviews stall Pillar C's network value, and a single reviewer is a bottleneck when away | Name a backup and set a 2-business-day target before Stage 4 |
| F3 | Coach verification evidence standard | Official staff-directory page on an https athletics site listing the person in a baseball role; valid 365 days | Stricter evidence slows onboarding; looser evidence lets non-coaches reach minors' profiles | Keep; add "institution email domain matches the program's official domain" as a reviewer checklist item in Stage 2 |
| F4 | Minors' coach-facing sharing needs a linked guardian's approval | **Decided 2026-10-21 by the founder: on** for discovery, academic summary, metrics, contact info, messaging and documents | Strong protection for 13–17-year-olds; adds friction that may slow early athlete discoverability | Keep on for launch; revisit with counsel and real funnel data (`consent_scope_rules.requires_guardian_approval_for_minor`) |
| F5 | What a linked parent/guardian sees by default | Only the basic athlete record; everything else needs the athlete's explicit grant | Respects athlete control; parents of minors may expect more | Keep explicit grants; at link approval, ask the athlete which items to share (no pre-checked boxes) |
| F6 | Can a minor end a parent/guardian link alone | Yes — either party can end a platform link | Platform access is consent-based, but parents of minors may object | Keep, pending counsel (L3) |
| F7 | Support and appeals channel, and the email address Base Recruits sends from | Not decided (founder unsure which address to use, 2026-10-22); UI copy says a support contact will be published before launch; `/support` explains the correction route meanwhile | Coaches need an appeal path; families need privacy-request handling; sign-up codes need a trusted sender | Use addresses on a Base Recruits domain (for example support@ and no-reply@), not a personal or other venture's address; decide before Stage 4 |
| F8 | Paid plan prices | **Updated 2026-10-25 by the founder:** Starter $9.99 a month or $99 a year, Plus $14.99 or $149, Pro $19.99 or $199 (annual saves about 17%); team packages proposed at $399 (10 athletes), $699 (20) and $1,499 (50) a year. Shown as proposals, not purchasable (`src/config/plans.ts`) | With discovery free (F19), Starter's paid content in the brief is thin (a recruiting timeline) | Before billing is built, decide what Starter includes beyond the free core, or offer two paid tiers |
| F9 | How strongly to verify that a "parent" is a parent | Self-attested adult age and relationship, plus the athlete's approval after seeing the account's name and email | Light verification keeps family onboarding easy; a determined teen with a second account and false answers could still pose as a parent | Keep for launch with audit monitoring; decide on stronger verification before coach messaging with minors (Stage 4) |
| F10 | When to let people change their account email | Off: the database refuses email-change requests | A change confirmed by a link or code sent to someone else's address could hand them that address (for a coach, a path toward a fake verification) | Keep off for launch. When added: confirm by code typed while signed in, and send coaches back through verification |
| F11 | Who approves registry imports | Any administrator can stage, review and publish; today that is the founder (Adam Concadoro). Every publish records who did it, with an optional note, and can be rolled back by restaging an earlier import | A single approver is simple but a bottleneck; a mistaken publish is visible on public pages until rolled back | Keep the founder as approver for launch. Before data partners or staff publish, require a second reviewer for any import that changes or removes more than 50 programs |
| F12 | Athletics association directories (NCAA, NAIA, NJCAA) | **Not used.** Division comes from the school's athletics classification in the federal EADA report; an administrator can correct it citing the association directory or the team's own site | Federal data covers every program in one public file, but its classification is institution-level: 37 programs are reported as "Other", and NCCAA/USCAA members may play baseball under another association | Work through the "Other" queue by hand before launch (admin → Schools and programs). Check each association's terms (L6) before automating any directory |
| F13 | How often the registry is refreshed | Manually: push a `data/*` branch (or run the data-refresh workflow on one), review the snapshot pull request, merge, then stage and publish in the app | EADA publishes once a year (autumn); College Scorecard updates a few times a year. Manual keeps a person in the loop | Refresh each autumn when EADA releases, and when Scorecard announces a data update. A scheduled workflow that only opens the pull request can come later |
| F14 | When search engines may index the site | **Off.** Every page sends `noindex` and `robots.txt` disallows crawling unless `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_ENV=production` and `ALLOW_INDEXING=true` | Indexing a pre-launch site with draft legal pages and an unconfirmed support route invites traffic the product can't yet serve; waiting delays search visibility | Turn on after legal review (L8), a support address (F7) and the custom domain |
| F15 | Fit labels in the score comparison (Strong / Competitive / Reach or similar) | **None shipped.** Results are grouped only by where scores sit against past-class ranges, with reasons; rules in `src/lib/matching/rules.ts` (`RULES_VERSION`) | Labels are what families expect from competitors, but without published thresholds and outcome data they read as admission predictions | Keep none until the founder approves a written rulebook with explicit thresholds; never derive thresholds with a language model |
| F16 | Registry data corrections from the October 2026 research (37 "Other" programs, Johns Hopkins testing policy) | **Decided 2026-10-23 by the founder:** applied the 12 strongest division corrections, the Johns Hopkins fall 2027 first-year testing policy (required), the LAI label for 8 Puerto Rico programs, and Southwest Virginia discontinued. The 8 weaker-evidence rows are held (`docs/remediation/proposed-data-corrections.md`) | Each correction changes public pages; weaker evidence risks a wrong division | Re-check the 8 held rows against current official pages before applying |
| F17 | Schools the federal athletics report leaves out (service academies, men's colleges, schools without federal aid, new teams) | **Decided 2026-10-25 by the founder:** add them by hand (admin → Schools and programs → Add a school), citing the official page; 13 approved and added on 2026-10-25 (`docs/remediation/data-check-2026-10-25.md`). They show no admissions figures or costs until the pipeline loads College Scorecard data for them | Hand-added schools need an administrator to re-check them each season; without them the registry misses Army, Navy, Air Force and other well-known programs | Keep. Next: have the data pipeline fetch College Scorecard figures for hand-added schools |
| F18 | Conference names for every program | **After launch (founder, 2026-10-25).** Conferences are filled in only when checked by hand | About 1,000 programs show no conference; the spreadsheets' conference lists come from Wikipedia and a third-party directory | Verify each conference's official member page and load them through a reviewed bulk import |
| F19 | What stays free, and when billing is built | **Decided 2026-10-25 by the founder:** school search, comparisons, saved schools and the score comparison stay free; paid plans sell new workflow tools only. Billing (Stripe subscriptions in test mode first) is built **after launch**; nothing is billed before the founder approves live payments | Free discovery is the reason families try and share the site; charging later depends on paid tools being worth it | Keep. Paying never changes search results, academic comparisons or coach visibility |
| F20 | First paid tool | **Decided 2026-10-25 by the founder:** a recruiting contact tracker and coach outreach planner (templates, deadline reminders), offered in Plus | An AI assistant was the alternative; it adds a per-use cost | Build after launch together with billing; add Pro tools one at a time as usage shows demand |
| F21 | When Assessment opens | **Decided 2026-10-26 by the founder:** built now on a branch, switched off, opened **after launch**. Two switches: the site (`NEXT_PUBLIC_ASSESSMENT_ENABLED`) and the database (admin page). `docs/assessment.md` | Opening with the launch adds support load and untested parent questions during the busiest weeks | Keep off through launch; open as a pilot with a small group first |
| F22 | Who reviews assessments (development coaches) | **Decided 2026-10-26 by the founder:** a new "Development coach" account type. Any adult can choose it; access to an athlete only through that athlete's one-time code, the athlete's approval and, under 18, a parent or guardian's approval. Coaching credentials are not checked | Self-selected coaches keep onboarding simple; without vetting, the guardian approval is the only check before an unknown adult sees a minor's clips | Before opening, decide who the first development coaches are (for example EA Baseball / ZT NY Westchester staff, confirmed with Jonathan) and whether to vet them like college coaches |
| F23 | Video uploads | **Decided 2026-10-26 by the founder:** private storage, **off** until legal review (L9). When on: 200 MB per clip, signed links, deletable, never sent to an analysis service | Video is the core evidence for timing and positions; minors' video raises retention, consent and safeguarding questions | Keep off until L9 is answered; then turn on for the pilot only. Raise the Supabase upload limit to 200 MB first |
| F24 | Reference benchmarks (percentiles) | **None shipped.** Administrators can load an approved dataset (≥ 30 distinct athletes, no synthetic data, exact metric/protocol/device/cohort match) | Percentiles are what families expect; invented or mismatched references would mislead | Load none until Base Recruits has its own consented data with a documented collection protocol |
| F25 | Sharing assessment reports with college coaches | Athlete shares published reports (never clips or drafts) with one verified college coach by email; guardian approval under 18; 365 days. A shared coach sees the current revision of every published report, including ones the development coach or a linked parent publishes | Reports may help recruiting conversations; any recruiting use must not imply the report predicts outcomes | Keep; review the wording with counsel (L11) before opening |
| F26 | Drill library and rubric wording | 18 written drills and 14 anchored rubrics drafted by engineering from general coaching practice; pitching drills never add throws | Content quality decides whether families trust the plans | Have a qualified hitting and pitching coach review every drill and anchor before opening |
| F27 | 9-Zone Hitting content | The workbook's drills, cues, constraints and delivery plan are shown as written, with their source rows; numbered references are not presented as verified sources; suggested volumes are planning guidance | The workbook is the founder's coaching framework; families will read it as Base Recruits' advice | Have a qualified hitting coach review the 39 drills, the zone cues and the Delivery Plan before opening, as for F26 |
| F28 | 9-Zone review prompts | Prompts only after ≥ 8 scored strike swings below 50% (simplify below 30%), or ≥ 10 scored decisions; coaches decide every stage change, with a reason | Higher minimums mean fewer, more reliable prompts; lower ones react faster but risk labelling a zone from a few swings | Keep for the pilot; revisit the numbers with the first development coaches' feedback |
| F29 | 9-Zone workbook download | Offered to signed-in users while Assessment is on the site (blank logs, no athlete data) | Useful as a paper backup at the field; the file leaves the site once downloaded | Keep; remove the link if the workbook becomes paid content |
| F38 | Hitting Development Workbook in the drill library | **Decided 2026-10-28 by the founder:** merge the workbook into the existing drill library (not a separate module). Its 66 drills were given new generic names and every outside organisation's name and link was removed. 40 hitting drills (H01–H40) and 6 rehearsals join the library; the band rehearsal (R04) waits for coach review. The swing-flaw guide (T01–T18) links each hitting measure to possible flaws and their drills, so suggestions name a possible cause, the check that tells causes apart, and the flaw's retest. Five starter drills are switched off where a workbook drill does the same job (plans already using them keep them). Success targets show as coach targets, not validated standards. Medicine-ball work, youth games, the readiness and power logs and the PDF macro are not imported | The workbook adds far more complete drills and the missing link from a result to a cause to a retest; families will read it as Base Recruits' advice, and without outside sources it rests on general coaching practice | Have a qualified hitting coach review the 46 drills, the targets and the flaw guide before opening, as for F26 and F27; confirm who wrote the workbook and that it may be published |
